## Vocabulary Glossary

Study these words and their meanings before attempting the exercises below. Each entry shows how the word was used in the passage.

| No. | Word/Expression | Meaning | Example from the passage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Epicentre** | The central or most intense point of a crisis | "Nigeria...the epicentre of the world's sickle cell burden." |
| 2 | **Scourge** | A cause of great suffering or trouble | "the sickle cell scourge." |
| 3 | **Genotype** | The genetic makeup of an individual | "know your genotype before you marry." |
| 4 | **Discordant** | Disagreeing or conflicting; not in harmony | "discordant or conflicting laboratory reports." |
| 5 | **Devolved** | Degenerated or declined into a worse state | "the medical laboratory ecosystem has devolved into guesswork." |
| 6 | **Reagents** | Substances used in chemical reactions or tests | "expired chemical reagents." |
| 7 | **Calibrated** | Adjusted or set to a standard of accuracy | "uncalibrated equipment" (i.e. not calibrated). |
| 8 | **Quackery** | Fraudulent claims of medical knowledge or skill | "this sprawling, informal network of medical quackery." |
| 9 | **Regulatory deficit** | A lack of proper rules, oversight, or enforcement | "a severe regulatory deficit compounds these issues." |
| 10 | **Compromised** | Weakened or made less reliable | "compromised materials." |
| 11 | **Stigma** | A mark of shame or disgrace associated with something | "the deep social stigmas surrounding the sickle cell trait." |
| 12 | **Cross-verification** | Checking information against another source to confirm it | "seek cross-verification from accredited, secondary reference laboratories." |
| 13 | **Accredited** | Officially recognised as meeting a required standard | "accredited, secondary reference laboratories." |
| 14 | **Chromatography** | A laboratory technique for separating mixtures | "High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)." |
| 15 | **Roulette wheel** *(expression)* | Something unpredictable, based purely on chance | "the laboratory has become a roulette wheel." |
| 16 | **Dumping ground** *(expression)* | A place where unwanted or substandard items are discarded | "Nigeria has become a dumping ground for uncalibrated equipment." |
| 17 | **Hollowed out** *(expression)* | Weakened from within; made ineffective | "a devastating systemic failure has hollowed out this defence." |
| 18 | **Beyond reproach** *(expression)* | Completely trustworthy; free from blame | "the science guiding citizens is absolutely beyond reproach." |
| 19 | **Bone-crushing** *(expression)* | Extremely severe or intense (pain) | "bone-crushing pain crises." |
| 20 | **Sprawling** | Spreading out over a large, often disorganised area | "this sprawling, informal network of medical quackery." |
| 21 | **Ecosystem** | A system or network of interconnected parts (used of an industry/sector) | "the medical laboratory ecosystem has devolved into guesswork." |
| 22 | **Prevalence** | How widespread or common something is within a population | "the already high prevalence of SCD." |
| 23 | **Advocacy** | Public support for or recommendation of a cause or policy | "stressed the need to enhance advocacy for stringent quality control measures." |
| 24 | **Premarital** | Occurring before marriage | "the little progress made in the area of premarital screening." |
| 25 | **Phase out** | To gradually stop using or remove something | "legally phase out primitive manual solubility testing." |
| 26 | **Penalise** | To punish someone for breaking a rule | "aggressively close down unaccredited facilities and penalise rogue practitioners." |
| 27 | **Systemic** | Relating to an entire system, not just isolated parts | "a devastating systemic failure." |
| 28 | **Gatekeepers** | People or institutions that control access to something | "how did the gatekeepers of Nigeria's diagnostic health become so unreliable?" |
| 29 | **Desperation** | A state of extreme need or hopelessness that drives risky action | "the desperation that drives individuals to bribe lab technicians." |
| 30 | **Catastrophic** | Extremely harmful or disastrous | "these laboratory errors continue to emerge in a rather catastrophic manner." |

## Section B: Fill in the Blanks

Choose the correct word/expression from the box below to complete each sentence. Use each word **only once**.

4. Without proper government oversight, the country has become a ________________ for fake and expired drugs.

5. Patients are advised to seek ________________ from a second, more reliable laboratory before trusting any single result.

6. Many quack laboratories operate without properly ________________ equipment, leading to wrong results.

7. Only results from ________________ laboratories should be trusted for something as serious as premarital screening.

9. For many couples, choosing a laboratory has become like spinning a ________________, since no one is sure the result will be accurate.

10. A severe ________________ has allowed thousands of unqualified laboratories to operate freely across the country.

11. When trusted institutions begin to fail the public, the entire system of protection has been ________________ from within.

23. Government agencies have promised to ________________ any laboratory found guilty of issuing fraudulent results.

24. The problem is not limited to a few bad laboratories; it is a ________________ failure affecting the entire testing process.

25. Medical laboratories are meant to serve as ________________ that protect the public from making uninformed health decisions.

26. Out of sheer ________________, some couples turn to unqualified laboratories that promise the results they want to hear.

27. The consequences of relying on fake results can be truly ________________ for affected families.
